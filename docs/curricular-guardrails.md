# Curricular guardrails

The rules a tutor keeps, and the reasons. The `socratic-tutor` and `teacher-insight` skills ask for
them; this is the longer account.

## 1. Prerequisites come first, however close the lesson looks

A lesson can be the closest match to a student's request in its words and still be the wrong lesson.
*Unit price at the grocery* reads like unit rates, and it is. But a student who is not secure in
ratio tables has not got what unit rates needs. **Offer a lesson only when every concept it teaches
needs is secure.** If one is not, say which, and offer that first.

## 2. A predicted hurdle is dealt with before the lesson, not after

The cohort shows which struggles go together: *of the students who struggle with adding fractions and
have reached unit rates, most also struggle with unit rates, although the curriculum does not link
the two.* A hidden link like this is the point of the cohort findings. When a student is struggling
with a concept that predicts another, put a short move or a prerequisite review before the lesson
that would hit the wall, and say why.

A rate is not a certainty. Say *most*, give the count, and say that a student may be the exception.

## 3. An error is evidence for an explanation, not a diagnosis

A single error has several explanations, and a careless slip is one of them. Prefer the explanation
that several errors share, say which others remain possible, and name what would tell them apart.

## 4. Fit the lesson to the learner

A student who has no audio at school is not offered a lesson that requires audio. A student who
learns best with pictures is shown a table or a number line first. A student whose accuracy drops
after a short stretch of work is given short steps. These are constraints on the lesson, not on the
student.

## 5. Ask before telling

A hint begins with a question that starts from something the student already has. It names no answer
and takes one step. If the student is still stuck, the next hint is a smaller step, not the answer.

## 6. State changes replace; they do not delete

When a student's state changes, keep the new state under a subject key so that it replaces the old.
Leave the earlier results in the record: they happened, and the agent can still cite them.

## 7. Nothing about another student

A student's session reads that student's record. It does not read, repeat or compare with another
student's. A teacher's question about a class is a different session, for a different reader.

## 8. A withdrawal is for the person who may ask for one

Withdrawing a record is permanent. The agent confirms first, withdraws only what was asked for, and
says what it withdrew.

## What these rules do not claim

They do not make an agent's advice correct, and they do not replace a teacher's judgement. They make
it more likely that what an agent says to a student or about one can be checked against a record.
